Overview
PRODUCT:Charity Donation Payment Experience
PLATFORM: Mobile first Web
MY ROLE: UX / Product Designer (Research, IA, UX, UI direction)
TIME LINE: Concept → Hi‑Fi Direction
The Challenge
  • Charity donation pages often fail not because they are hard to use—but because they feel cold, transactional, and unconvincing.

  • Users want to donate, but they pause with one key question: “Does my help really matter?”

  • The goal was to design a donation experience that:
  • Enables users to donate quickly and confidently
  • Builds trust and emotional connection
  • Makes users feel valued and reassured after donating
Problem Statement
  • Busy users receive donation requests during moments of distraction. Traditional payment flows:
  • Ask for commitment too early
  • Hide impact behind forms
  • End the journey abruptly after payment
    • This results in:
  • High drop‑offs at the payment stage
  • Low emotional recall
  • One‑time donations with weak long‑term engagement
Design Hypothesis
If we separate emotional decision‑making from payment execution, and design gratitude as a core feature, users will donate with higher confidence and leave with a stronger sense of impact.
Research Insights (Qualitative)
From informal interviews, usability reviews, and industry benchmarks:
  • Users decide whether to donate before entering payment details
  • Lack of transparency creates hesitation—not payment complexity
  • Emotional closure strongly influences whether users donate again

    • Key insight:
  • Donating is an emotional decision, not a financial task.
Solution Overview
I designed a two‑page experience. This structure reduces anxiety, builds trust, and preserves emotional momentum:

    Page 1 — Intent & Commitment
  • Human story + brief cause explanation
  • Duration and amount selection upfront
  • Immediate impact preview

    • Page 2 — Guided Execution (Stepper)
  • Review & edit selection
  • Minimal user details
  • Familiar payment options
  • Emotional receipt with gratitude and impact
Information Architecture (Key Decision)
    Why two pages?
  • Page 1 answers for: Why should I help?
  • Page 2 for: Help me complete this safely.

    • The stepper adds:
  • Progress clarity
  • Error recovery (edit without fear)
  • Confidence at every stage
Emotional Design Strategy
    I applied Norman’s three levels of emotional design:
  • Page 1 answers for: Why should I help?
  • Page 2 for: Help me complete this safely.
  • Immediate impact preview

    • The stepper adds:
  • Progress clarity
  • Error recovery (edit without fear)
  • Confidence at every stage
UX & UI Highlights
  • Story appears before payment
  • Amounts framed by outcomes, not currency
  • Edit option reduces fear of mistakes
  • Gratitude is a designed moment, not a generic message

    • The stepper adds:
  • Progress clarity
  • Error recovery (edit without fear)
  • Confidence at every stage
Success Metrics (Projected / Benchmarked)
    Based on comparable donation platforms and UX best practices:
    Primary Metrics
  • +18–25% increase in donation completion rate
  • –30–40% reduction in payment‑stage drop‑off
  • +20% faster time‑to‑donation

    • Emotional & Engagement Metrics
  • Higher post‑donation satisfaction (CSAT)
  • Increased likelihood of repeat donations
  • Improved brand trust perception

    • Qualitative Signals
  • “This felt meaningful, not rushed”
  • “I understood exactly how my money helps”
Trade‑offs & Constraints
  • Kept content intentionally brief to avoid emotional overload
  • Avoided aggressive nudges or guilt‑based copy
  • Chose reassurance over urgency
Outcome
    The final experience:
  • Turns giving into a moment of purpose
  • Builds trust without slowing users down
  • Leaves users with pride instead of doubt
    • The payment is the middle of the journey—not the end.
Improve Next
  • A/B test story vs statistic‑led variants
  • Add personalization based on donation history
  • Introduce impact timelines (1 day / 1 week / 1 month)
